# Errata — *Faceless* course (Higgsfield, ID Premium)

For the author. This file lists where the course does not hold for a preschool channel, and what to
change. It does not replace the course. Section titles are quoted in Indonesian so they can be found
in the book; page numbers are from the 151-page PDF.

**What holds.** The G1–G4 gates, the process milestones instead of income deadlines, the BEP table,
the written loss limit, and the rule that a policy number must come from the source are all good. We
adopted them. The errata below are about coverage and about three instructions that are wrong on a
made-for-kids channel.

## E1. The production stack is aimed at the wrong format — Modules 2, 3, 12

The course assumes a talking avatar: an avatar bible, character consistency, a Higgsfield clip per
line, and a credit budget per minute (page 110, page 117). That is the right stack for a narrator
channel with a presenter.

A preschool animation channel does not use a talking avatar. The characters are drawn, the mouth is
two lines, and the motion is simple. Two consequences:

- The avatar bible and the credit arithmetic do not transfer. A 3-minute episode is drawn once and
  reused across a season, so the marginal cost per episode is voice, music, and edit time, not clips.
- A course module for this format is missing: character sheet, expression set, background set, and a
  reusable scene kit. That is the actual production asset for animation, and it is cheaper than the
  avatar path.

**Change.** Add a short branch in Module 3: "animated format" versus "presenter format", with the
asset list for each. Say early which niches need which.

## E2. End screens, cards, and pinned comments do not exist on made-for-kids — Module 8, pages 76 and 110

The checklist says: "Subtitle, end screen, cards — tambahkan subtitle dan arahkan ke video terkait",
and the discovery list says "gunakan playlist, end screen, dan komentar yang disematkan".

On a video set as made for kids, YouTube disables end screens, cards, comments, the save-to-playlist
button, memberships, and notifications. The advice cannot be followed, and a producer who builds an
end screen into the master wastes the last 20 seconds of the render.

**Change.** Add one line to Module 8: "If the video is made for kids, skip end screens, cards, and
pinned comments. Put the next-video pointer in the spoken recap and in the description instead." Add
the same line to the release checklist.

## E3. The audience field is a legal attestation, not a preference — page 76, page 110, page 122

The course says: "Pilih sesuai kenyataan apakah dibuat untuk anak". That is correct but not enough.
The field is an attestation under COPPA and the FTC's children's privacy rule, and the consequences
are large:

- No personalised ads, so the RPM is lower. The BEP table must use the made-for-kids RPM.
- No comments, end screens, cards, or memberships.
- A wrong setting is a compliance risk for the channel, not only a settings mistake.
- Content that targets children but claims a 13+ audience is a policy violation.

**Change.** Replace the one-line instruction with a four-line box: what the setting disables, what it
does to revenue, who is legally responsible, and that the setting is chosen at upload and checked by
a second person.

## E4. The monetisation table is out of date, and the caveat is now resolved — pages 43, 77, 117

The table on page 43 gives the entry tier as 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10 million
Shorts views. Page 117 correctly refuses to state the 2027 change without a source.

The source now exists. YouTube announced on 10 August 2026 that from **1 February 2027** new
applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus **8,000** watch hours in 365 days, or **20 million** Shorts
views in 90 days. Existing partners keep the old threshold.

**Change.** Update the page 43 table with two rows, "until 31 January 2027" and "from 1 February
2027", and cite the YouTube blog post. Delete the caveat on page 117. Also make the two tables agree:
the detailed table on page 77 already lists the fan-funding tier (500 subscribers, 3,000 hours, 3
million Shorts views) and the page 43 table does not.

## E5. Multi-channel advice conflicts with the inauthentic-content policy — Module 7, page 69

The course recommends several channels with a separate niche and style for each. That is reasonable
for a presenter format. For a kids channel it is the fastest route to a channel-level action:
mass-produced, templated children's content is the exact pattern the inauthentic-content policy and
the kids quality principles name. Low-quality made-for-kids content can lose ads and can remove a
channel from the Partner Program.

**Change.** Add a stop rule to Module 7: "One kids channel at a time. Do not open a second until the
first has passed five episodes in a row with no compliance waiver." Keep the multi-channel advice for
the presenter niches.

## E6. The BEP table uses a cost base that is too low for animation — Module 5, page 57

The break-even rows on page 57 are arithmetically correct. The input is too small for this format.
The ad-only row of 58,000 / 29,000 / 19,333 views per month implies a fixed cost of about **USD 29**
(one Pro plan) and an RPM of USD 0.50 to 1.50. It leaves out a licensed voice, an illustration set,
and a composer.

With a realistic kids cost of USD 150 a month at USD 1.00 RPM, break-even is **150,000 views a
month**, not 29,000. A reader who plans against the table will read a normal first month as a
failure.

**Change.** Add a second table for the animated format with the real line items, or add one sentence:
"Add the voice, art, and music lines before you use this table."

## E7. Missing: music rights and Content ID — Module 3 and Module 10

The course covers voice rights and synthetic-media disclosure well. It does not cover the largest
claim risk on a kids channel: a nursery rhyme. A recording taken from a streaming service, a sample
pack, or a "free" library can be claimed by another owner's Content ID, and a claim on a made-for-kids
video is slow and public to resolve.

**Change.** Add a paragraph and a small table: original composition, library track with a
monetisation clause, public-domain melody with our own recording, and the four fields to record
(title, source, licence with the quoted clause, Content ID status, date checked).

## E8. Missing: the kids quality principles — Module 4

The course covers the inauthentic-content policy. It does not mention YouTube's separate quality
principles for made-for-kids content, which can limit ads and remove a channel from the Partner
Program even when no rule was broken.

**Change.** Add the five or six questions from the principles as a checklist: does it teach, is it
age-appropriate, is it calm, is it original, does it avoid keyword stuffing and surprise-egg formats.

## Order of work

If only three edits are possible, make them in this order: **E3** (the legal field), **E4** (the
threshold), **E2** (the end screen instruction). The first prevents a compliance fault, the second
prevents a wrong plan, the third prevents wasted production on every episode.
